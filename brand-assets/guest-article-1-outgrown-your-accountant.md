## Article

Ask a room of small business owners when they last reviewed their accountant, and most will look at their shoes. We switch energy suppliers, insurers and banks without a second thought, yet we stay loyal to an accountant we may have outgrown years ago. Usually it comes down to one mistaken belief: that changing is more hassle than it's worth. It rarely is. Here's how to tell if your accountant is holding you back, and what to do if they are.

### The signs worth noticing

A good accountant is one of the most valuable relationships a business has. A poor one costs you quietly, in time, stress and tax you didn't need to pay. The warning signs are usually there if you look for them:

- You only ever hear from them at the deadline, never before it.
- You're the one chasing, for answers, for figures, for a call back.
- They file what you send and nothing more: no advice, no planning, no "have you thought about…".
- The bill arrives as a surprise, with no warning.
- Your business has changed, taking on staff, going limited, crossing the VAT threshold, and they haven't kept pace.

One of these on its own might just need a frank conversation. Several together is a pattern, and a pattern is worth acting on.

### Why we stay too long

Three beliefs keep owners stuck. The first is that switching is a huge administrative headache. The second is that it will cost a fortune, or mean paying two firms at once. The third is a vague sense of loyalty, or simple inertia.

All three are worth challenging. Your new accountant does almost all of the work of moving. In most cases the changeover itself costs nothing. And loyalty should run both ways: if a firm isn't giving your business the attention it deserves, staying put is loyalty to your own discomfort, not to a relationship.

### What good actually looks like

Before you decide anything, it helps to know what you're aiming for. A firm worth having is proactive rather than reactive: they contact you through the year with things to consider, not just at filing time. They're responsive, with a named person who replies. They're clear on price, so the bill is never a shock. And they give you advice you can act on, explained in plain English, rather than a set of accounts you file and forget.

If that sounds a long way from what you have now, it may be time to look.

### How switching actually works

This is the part that surprises people. Switching accountants is mostly a formality, and your new firm leads it. You choose the new accountant first, and only then give notice to the old one. Your new firm writes to your old one for "professional clearance", a routine letter, and requests your records, which your old firm is obliged to hand over. You authorise the new firm with HMRC, and that's largely it.

The active work on your side is an hour or two, spread over a couple of weeks, and most switches complete comfortably within a month. You can do it at any point in the year, too. There's no need to wait for your year-end.

### The hardest part isn't the process

The hardest part of switching accountants isn't the paperwork. It's simply deciding to do it. But the cost of staying with the wrong firm compounds every year, in missed advice, avoidable tax and time you won't get back. If you recognise your own business in the signs above, the good news is that putting it right is far easier than you fear.
